\underline{\textbf{Solution for 10}}

$\Delta \vec{V} = \left\langle \vec{a} \right\rangle \Delta t$

and $\vec{a}$ is provided by $f_s$ from ground.

For a given $\Delta \vec{V}$, $\Delta t$ is minimum when

$a_{\max} = \mu g$ is along $\Delta \vec{V}$ continuously.

So the minimal time for his turn is

$t = \frac{\left|\vec{v_2} - \vec{v_1}\right|}{\mu g} = \frac{\sqrt{2}v}{\mu g} \approx 7s$

The boy's acceleration vector is constant throughout, with magnitude $\mu g$, and directed to the southeast (it makes an angle of $45^{\circ}$ with the x-axis), and so his trajectory during the turn (in the same way as for projectiles) is a parabola.

The parabola has its axis in the northwest-southeast direction,

Path equation in $x'-y'$ frame is

$y' = x'\tan 45^{\circ} - \frac{a(x')^2}{2v_0^2\cos^2 45^{\circ}} \Rightarrow y' = x' - \frac{(\mu g)(x')^2}{2 \times 5^2 \times \frac{1}{2}}$

$y' = x' - \frac{1}{25}(x')^2$ --- ①

From figure, $x = \frac{x'}{\sqrt{2}} - \frac{y'}{\sqrt{2}} \Rightarrow x = \frac{x' - y'}{\sqrt{2}}$

and $y = \frac{x' + y'}{\sqrt{2}}$

--- ③ ② + ③

$\Rightarrow x + y = \sqrt{2}x' \Rightarrow x' = \frac{x + y}{\sqrt{2}}$

substitute these ③ - ②

$\Rightarrow y - x = \sqrt{2}y' \Rightarrow y' = \frac{y - x}{\sqrt{2}}$ two in ①

$\frac{y - x}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{y + x}{\sqrt{2}} - \frac{1}{25}\left(\frac{x + y}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^2$

$\sqrt{2}x = \frac{1}{25} \cdot \frac{(x + y)^2}{2} \Rightarrow x + y = \sqrt{50\sqrt{2}x}$

$y = \sqrt{25 \times 2\sqrt{2}x} - x$

$y = 5\left(2^{3/4}\right)\sqrt{x} - x$

Equation of path just like normal projectile is
